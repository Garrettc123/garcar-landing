# Garcar Digital — Email Launch Campaigns

**Status:** Draft copy only. Nothing has been sent. Do not upload contacts or send until the sender identity, consent source, unsubscribe mechanism, and suppression list are checked.

## Operating rules
- Email only people who have a lawful basis to receive the message; use explicit opt-in for marketing lists.
- Use accurate sender identity and subject lines. Include a valid physical postal address and a clear unsubscribe method in every marketing email.
- Honor opt-outs promptly; never buy, scrape, or cold-blast personal email lists.
- Do not imply a testimonial, clinical result, income result, scientific proof, scarcity, or endorsement that cannot be documented.
- Use a separate audience tag for each market. Do not infer sensitive interests from behavior.
- Send no email automatically until a human approves the campaign and test message.

## Shared 5-message sequence
1. **Welcome / deliver the promised free sample.** Subject: Your Garcar guide preview. Explain what was requested, deliver the sample, and set expectations.
2. **Useful lesson.** Subject: One idea you can put to work today. Teach one small exercise; no hard sell.
3. **Common mistake.** Subject: The shortcut that usually backfires. Correct a misconception relevant to the market.
4. **Product introduction.** Subject: A practical guide for your next step. Describe contents, format, price, limitations, and who it is for.
5. **Decision email.** Subject: Is this guide right for you? Recap deliverables, answer objections honestly, and provide a clear opt-out.

## Market-specific message angles

### Inner Awareness — The Third Eye: Science & Spirituality ($27 proposed)
- Email 1: Why the pineal gland attracts so much attention; separate biology from symbolism.
- Email 2: A 30-second pause: breathe comfortably, notice three sensations, and write one observation. This is a mindfulness exercise, not a gland activation.
- Email 3: The difference between a meaningful spiritual metaphor and a scientific claim.
- Email 4: Introduce the guide: pineal anatomy, cultural symbolism, evidence review, reflection prompts, and a sleep-friendly routine.
- Email 5: Invite the reader to decide whether a balanced science-and-symbolism guide fits their curiosity.

### Wealth & Abundance — The Abundance Blueprint ($37 proposed)
- Email 1: Welcome and ask the reader to define what “financially secure” means in measurable terms.
- Email 2: The 15-minute money snapshot: income, essential bills, minimum debt payments, and next due dates.
- Email 3: Why positive thinking is not a substitute for a cash-flow plan.
- Email 4: Introduce the guide: goals, spending review, emergency buffer planning, skill-building, and weekly review.
- Email 5: Explain the product is educational, not investment advice or a promise of income.

### Mind & Performance — The Mind Reprogramming Method ($29 proposed)
- Email 1: Welcome and choose one behavior to make easier this week.
- Email 2: Use an implementation intention: “When [cue] happens, I will [small action].”
- Email 3: Reduce friction rather than relying on motivation alone.
- Email 4: Introduce the guide: attention audit, cue design, focus blocks, habit tracking, and review.
- Email 5: Explain the workbook is a practice tool, not a medical or mental-health treatment.

### Human Connection — The Connection Blueprint ($27 proposed)
- Email 1: Welcome and identify one conversation the reader wants to handle more clearly.
- Email 2: Practice reflective listening: summarize what you heard before responding.
- Email 3: A boundary is a clear statement of what you will do, not a tool to control another person.
- Email 4: Introduce the guide: listening, emotional naming, repair attempts, boundaries, and conversation prompts.
- Email 5: Explain the guide supports communication practice; it cannot guarantee a relationship outcome or replace professional help.
